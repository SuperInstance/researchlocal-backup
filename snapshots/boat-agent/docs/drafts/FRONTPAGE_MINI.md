# boat-agent

*For the working deck. Not a tech project. A second mate.*

---

It's 11:40, you're running gear, gloves on, the rail's wet with chum. You say "twelve chum over the rail" and keep working. Later, running back to the spot, you ask "how was last week off the point?" and the answer is right there. You didn't write anything down. You didn't open a laptop. You talked.

That's the whole point of this thing.

You hire a deckhand who never sleeps, never complains, and never touches the wheel without asking. He watches. He remembers. He writes it down so you don't have to. When he's sure, he suggests. When he's not, he asks. The wheel is still yours. Always.

---

## What you get, plain

**1. A mate with perfect memory.** Talk to him like a deckhand. Mid-set, gloves on. He writes everything down as a transcript, stamped with the time and the GPS spot in the file name. Ask him anything later — "how was last week off the point?" "what did the sounder look like Tuesday dawn?" "show me every time I said birds working last month." He searches it all. The model doesn't matter. The memory does.

**2. A mate who watches your screens.** The sounder, the plotter, the numbers — he's got eyes on them. Every minute he notes what's changed. Every ten minutes he saves a short record. Once an hour he sends a one-screen summary to your phone. End of trip, a full debrief. You never save a file. You never name a folder. He does. Because the location's in the filename, you can ask "show me every Tuesday off the rock" and he groups it for you. The sounder joins the conversation.

**3. A mate with eyes in the engine room.** A cam facing forward for logs, buoys, boats coming at you in the dark. An IR cam in the engine room for hot spots, leaks, the bilge rising. Early warning while you're sleeping or running gear.

**4. A mate you call by name.** Say the word, he's listening. Say it again, he's yours. Say his name back, he's off. No buttons to find with wet gloves.

**5. A mate you build with.** Need auto-throttle for trolling? Tell him. He interviews you — cable or hydraulic? Micro Commander? chain and sprocket? cable lengths? — and you two plan it together. He helps you pick the parts. He helps you order them. He writes the code *and* the wiring diagram. You're the hands. He's the brain. The boat gets a new trick and you own every nut on it.

---

## What he will NEVER do

- Drive without you at the wheel.
- Override your hand on the wheel or the throttle.
- Delete a day's record without reading it once.
- Push your boat's data anywhere without you saying so first.
- Pretend he knows when he doesn't.

If you grab the wheel, he goes quiet and politely asks why. Out loud. Even a grunt is teaching him.

---

## Install

1. Plug the black box into the helm NMEA network and power.
2. Stick the forward cam on the bow rail, the IR cam in the engine room.
3. Open `boat-agent.local` on the pilot-house tablet. Pair your phone.
4. Set the dial to **1 — Coach**. Go fishing.
5. Talk. He listens. After a trip or two, move the dial when you're ready.

Slow is fine. The boat is yours.

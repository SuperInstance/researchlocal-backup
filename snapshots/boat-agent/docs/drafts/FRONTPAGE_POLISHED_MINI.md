# boat-agent

**A deckhand with perfect memory. Talks back. Never sleeps. Works on your boat today.**

*For the working deck. Not a tech project. A second mate.*

---

It's 1 PM, you're running gear, gloves on, the rail's wet with chum. You don't stop. You just say it:

**"Twelve chum over the rail. Two of them bruisers."**

A week later you're idling in the same water, coffee in hand: **"How'd we do here last Tuesday?"** It tells you — what you caught, when the bite turned, the tide, the sounder. You didn't write a thing. You didn't open a laptop. You talked.

## What you get, plain

**1. A mate with perfect memory.** Talk like a deckhand, mid-set. He writes it down as a transcript — time and GPS spot in the file name. Ask him anything later: "how was last week off the point?" "sounder Tuesday dawn?" "every time I said birds working last month." The memory is the product, and the memory lives on your boat.

**2. A mate who watches your screens.** He does what you do — stare at the sounder all day, keep the picture. Every minute he logs what changed. Every ten minutes he saves a short record. Hourly, a one-screen summary on your phone. End of trip, the full debrief. Because the spot's in the filename, "every Tuesday off the rock" just works. The logbook writes itself.

**3. A mate with eyes in the engine room.** A cam forward — logs, buoys, boats coming at you in the dark. A second set of eyes when you're on the gear, not the water. An IR cam in the engine room — hot spots before they're failures, a leak before it's a story, bilge creeping up at 3 AM. It texts you. "Any alarms last night?" is a question you can ask over coffee.

**4. A mate you call by name.** Say the word, he's listening. **"Take the helm"** — he steers. **"I've got her"** — you're driving. No buttons with wet gloves. Your hand on the wheel or the throttle outranks everything he thinks, every time. That's the design.

**5. A mate you build with.** Need the throttle to handle itself while you troll — easing when you turn into the wind, back down when you fall off? Tell him. He interviews you like a sharp deckhand would — cable or hydraulic? Micro Commander? chain and sprocket? run length? — and you two sketch the plan. He picks the parts, orders them, and when the box lands he hands you a wiring diagram drawn for YOUR boat and code you can read, with the reasons written in. You wrench it in. You're the hands; he's the head. A boat that trolls the way YOU troll, because he learned from watching you do it. Next season, when something needs adjusting, you just ask.

---

## What he will NEVER do

- Drive without you at the wheel.
- Override your hand on the wheel or the throttle.
- Delete a day's record without reading it once.
- Push your boat's data anywhere without you saying so.
- Pretend he knows when he doesn't.

You grab the wheel, he goes quiet and asks why. Out loud. Even a grunt is teaching him.

---

## Getting started

1. **Install the app** on the boat's laptop (Windows, one installer).
2. **Plug in your GPS** — and the sounder when you're ready for level 2.
3. **Say something.** "Start the log." That's it.

The dial starts at **1 — Coach**: he suggests, you decide. Move it up trip by trip. Slow is fine.

Cameras, the autopilot, and the build-it-together part come later, when you want them. Everything works offline; the cloud is a backup, not a requirement.

*If something here doesn't match what the boat does, the boat is right and this page is wrong. Tell us.*
